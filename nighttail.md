# NIGHTTAIL: The Hollow Crown

**Yes, mate—the combination works: Zero-style speed and combat, a gothic castle, and a cast of monkey heroes.** The strongest version is an original game, not a scene-for-scene remake.

Below are the research findings, the complete **Story 1 campaign plan**, plans for **Stories 2–4**, and a **playable first build**.

**Scope, honestly:** the build is a short mechanics prototype with a beginning, three areas, a boss, and an ending. It is **not yet the finished one-hour campaign**.

## 1. What to take from the research

| Reference | What matters | Our version |
|---|---|---|
| **Mega Man Zero 4 — combat** | Sword and ranged combat, plus the Zero Knuckle’s temporary enemy-weapon stealing and environmental interactions. [2][19][21] | Fast blade combat, ranged darts, dash movement, and stealing a weakened guard’s weapon. |
| **Mega Man Zero 4 — campaign** | A base, selectable boss missions, then final stages—not a single continuous castle. [2][19] | A castle sanctuary with connected wings. The full campaign opens into a choice of routes after its introduction. |
| **Mega Man Zero 4 — weather** | Weather changes stage difficulty and conditions; harder conditions allow boss EX-skill rewards. [19][21] | **Moon states:** Pale Moon for the standard route; Blood Moon for harder encounters and optional combat rewards. |
| **Mega Man Zero 4 — progression** | One upgradeable Cyber Elf, enemy-part crafting, and unlockable special attacks. [2][21] | One lantern companion, a small readable relic system, and boss-earned techniques. |
| **Castlevania: Symphony of the Night** | Castle exploration, combat, and areas opened by newly acquired abilities. [8][20] | Gothic architecture, looping shortcuts, secrets, and movement-gated routes—but retain fast combat. |
| **Bloons TD 6** | Monkey heroes, upgrades, and activated abilities are central to its identity. [16] | An original monkey-hero team with distinct weapons and powers—not copied hero names, costumes, or dialogue. |

**Important:** a contemporary Zero 4 reviewer reported roughly five hours for a first completion. Our one-hour campaign should be a deliberately smaller original adventure, not the whole game compressed into an hour. [21]

The research here used written reviews, a detailed gameplay guide, and official product pages. **I have not watched or personally played an entire Zero 4 playthrough during this task.**

## 2. The game we are building

### Core identity

**Fast action first. Castle exploration second.**

You play **Rook**, a monkey courier carrying a broken royal blade. His sister **Mira**, a bell engineer, has disappeared inside a fortress that steals the voices of nearby villages.

At midnight, its largest bell will ring. Everyone whose voice it holds will become a soldier of the Hollow Crown.

### Original hero cast

| Hero | Role | Story 1 use |
|---|---|---|
| **Rook** | Agile sword-and-dart courier | Playable protagonist |
| **Mira** | Engineer using sound and clockwork | Missing sister; central to the plot |
| **Sable** | Lantern-bearing wilderness mystic | Sanctuary guide and companion upgrades |
| **Brann** | Heavy gauntlet defender | Protects rescued villagers |
| **Iri** | Precise longbow scout | Opens shortcuts and reports enemy movements |

**For the first campaign, only Rook is playable.** This keeps the movement, combat, animation, and level design focused.

### Combat rules

- Responsive running and jumping.
- Ground and aerial blade attacks.
- Ranged darts for safer pressure.
- Short dash with a cooldown.
- Enemy attacks that visibly wind up before striking.
- Weakened ordinary enemies can surrender a temporary weapon.
- Bosses test movement and attack recognition—not inflated health.
- Death returns you to a checkpoint without deleting permanent progress.

**Later production work:** wall sliding, wall jumping, charged attacks, blade combos, and configurable controls. These are not all implemented in the prototype below.

## 3. Story 1 — *The Hollow Crown*

### Full story

The fortress of **Vesper Keep** has begun collecting voices. Villagers wake unable to speak, while masked soldiers appear at its gates.

Rook enters to find Mira, believing the fortress has taken her prisoner.

Inside, he discovers that its commander, **the Bell Regent**, once built the keep as a refuge during a terrible winter. To preserve its dying inhabitants, he bound their voices into a machine called the **Mercy Engine**.

The machine kept them moving—but took away their freedom.

Now the Regent plans to “save” every nearby village in the same way.

Mira entered voluntarily to stop him. She has learned that destroying the engine while the voices remain trapped will destroy the people bound to it. Instead, she needs Rook to break three restraints, return the voices, and defend her while she reverses the engine.

Rook defeats the Regent. Mira frees the villagers. They destroy the engine only after its prisoners have escaped.

**The first story ends properly:** the villages are saved, Mira survives, and the keep falls silent.

Then the broken crown sends one final signal across the sea. Something answers.

### One-hour campaign budget

These are **design targets, not measured playtimes**.

| Chapter | Target | Gameplay and story |
|---|---:|---|
| **1. The Silent Causeway** | 6 min | Teach movement, blade, darts, and dash. Find the first voiceless survivor. |
| **2. Ashen Cloister** | 9 min | Learn weapon stealing. Defeat the Censer Knight and reach the sanctuary. |
| **3. Rootbound Ossuary** | 9 min | Rescue Sable. Earn a movement relic and open the first major shortcut. |
| **4. The Drowned Belfry** | 10 min | Platforming and ranged pressure. Defeat the Ferryman of Brass. |
| **5. The Glass Conservatory** | 9 min | Recombine movement skills. Find Mira and learn the engine’s purpose. |
| **6. Crownworks / Mercy Engine** | 12 min | Break the final restraint, defend Mira, and fight the Bell Regent. |
| **7. Escape and resolution** | 5 min | Return through a changed entrance route; save the last survivors; ending. |
| **Total** | **60 min** | No forced waiting or mandatory grinding. |

After the cloister, the ossuary and belfry can be tackled in either order. Both reconnect to the sanctuary.

### Boss roster

1. **Censer Knight:** swinging censer, low fire wave, exposed recovery.
2. **Root Matron:** thorn lanes, hanging seed pods, vulnerable heart.
3. **Ferryman of Brass:** lunges, rising water platforms, thrown anchors.
4. **Bell Regent:** deliberate blade attacks and bell projectiles.
5. **Mercy Engine:** a final encounter built around freeing prisoners and exposing its core.

### What makes the hour worthwhile

- Each major area introduces a different challenge.
- New abilities reveal useful shortcuts, not long compulsory backtracking.
- Optional secrets reward exploration without blocking the ending.
- The story resolves its main conflict.
- Blood Moon challenges add replay value after the main path works.

## 4. Stories 2, 3, and 4

| Game | Story | Main gameplay addition | Complete ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2 — The Saltglass Sea** | The crown’s signal reaches a fleet carrying stolen memories. Rook and Iri pursue its admiral through a drowned coastal city. | Iri becomes playable at designated sanctuaries; tides change routes; aerial weapon steals. | The fleet is stopped and its captives recover their identities. The source of the crown technology is discovered. |
| **3 — The Sun Beneath** | The heroes descend into the buried city that created the Mercy Engine. Its surviving ruler offers to erase suffering by erasing choice. | Brann’s heavy combat, destructible barriers, and paired environmental puzzles. | The city’s control network is dismantled. The heroes learn that the crowns were built to contain a much older threat. |
| **4 — The Last Dawn** | The broken network releases the First Silence, which consumes language, memory, and eventually identity. The team must replace the old system without sacrificing anyone to it. | The established heroes return in authored sections; their abilities combine in the finale. | The First Silence is contained through a freely maintained alliance, not a captive power source. Rook and Mira return home. |

**Series rule:** every game finishes its own story. Sequel hooks must not replace an ending.

## 5. First playable build

### Included

- Three connected side-scrolling areas.
- Running, jumping, dash, blade, and ranged attacks.
- Weapon stealing from weakened guards.
- A boss with a visible attack windup and a second phase.
- Keyboard and touch controls.
- Area-start checkpoint saving when browser storage is available.
- Separate title, pause, settings, defeat, and ending screens.
- Original geometric placeholder artwork.

### Controls

**Move:** A/D or arrows · **Jump:** Space · **Blade:** J · **Shoot:** K  
**Steal:** L · **Dash:** Shift · **Pause:** Escape

**Stealing:** hit a guard twice with the blade, approach it, then press **L**. Boss weapons cannot be stolen.
